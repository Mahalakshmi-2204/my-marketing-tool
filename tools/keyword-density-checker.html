Frequently asked questions

What is keyword density?

Keyword density is the percentage of your text taken up by a word or phrase. It is calculated by dividing the number of times the keyword appears by the total number of words, then multiplying by 100.

What is the ideal keyword density?

There is no ideal number, and Google has not published a target. Many writers use roughly 1 to 2 percent as a loose guide, but it is more important to write naturally and cover the topic well.

Can too much keyword repetition hurt my rankings?

Yes. Repeating a keyword unnaturally is called keyword stuffing, and Google's spam policies list it as a violation. It also makes your writing harder to read.

What does "ignore common words" do?

It hides everyday words like the, and, of and is from the results, so your real topic words and phrases stand out. Turn it off to count every word.

Should I use one keyword or related phrases?

Use your main keyword where it fits naturally, plus related words and variations. The two-word and three-word tabs help you see which phrases you already use often.

Does this tool store my text?

No. The analysis runs entirely in your browser, and nothing you paste is sent to a server or saved.
