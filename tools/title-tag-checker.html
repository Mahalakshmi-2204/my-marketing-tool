Frequently asked questions

What is a title tag?

The title tag is the HTML element that sets your page's title. It's the clickable headline in Google results and the text shown on the browser tab. It's one of the strongest on-page signals for telling search engines and searchers what a page is about.

What is the ideal title tag length?

Aim for roughly 30 to 60 characters. Google actually truncates titles by pixel width (around 600px) rather than character count, so titles with wide letters like "W" or "M" may cut off earlier. Treat 60 as a safe guideline, not a hard limit.

What happens if my title is too long?

Google may cut it off with an ellipsis, hiding words at the end. Google can also rewrite titles it considers too long, vague or stuffed with keywords. Put your most important words at the beginning.

What if my title is too short?

A very short title often leaves out context or keywords that help searchers decide to click. Adding a descriptor or your brand name usually fixes it.

Should I include my brand name in the title?

Usually yes, at the end, separated by a pipe or dash (for example "Free SEO Tools | GrowthKit"). Put your primary keyword first and your brand last, and leave the brand out if it pushes the title over the limit.

Does every page need a unique title?

Yes. Duplicate titles make it harder for search engines to tell your pages apart and can lead to the wrong page ranking for a query.

Does this tool store my title?

No. The analysis runs entirely in your browser, and nothing you type is sent to a server or saved.
