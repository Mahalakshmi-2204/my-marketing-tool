Frequently asked questions

What is engagement rate?

Engagement rate is the percentage of your audience that interacts with your content. It is calculated by dividing the total interactions, such as likes, comments, shares and saves, by your audience size, then multiplying by 100.

How do I calculate engagement rate?

Add up the interactions on your post or posts, divide by your followers, reach or impressions, and multiply by 100. If you are measuring several posts against followers, divide the total by the number of posts as well to get an average per post.

What is a good engagement rate?

There is no single good number. It varies by platform, industry and account size, and smaller accounts often see higher rates. As a loose guide for rates based on followers, under 1 percent is low, 1 to 3 percent is average, and above that is strong. Comparing against your own past results is usually more useful than comparing against other accounts.

What is the difference between followers, reach and impressions?

Followers is how many people follow your account. Reach is how many different people saw your content. Impressions or views count every time your content was shown, including repeat views by the same person. Rates based on reach or impressions are usually higher than rates based on followers, so only compare like with like.

Which interactions should I count?

Most people count likes, comments, shares and saves. Be consistent: use the same set of interactions every time so your results stay comparable. Link clicks and video views are sometimes counted too, but this tool uses the four above.

Does this tool store my numbers?

No. The calculation runs entirely in your browser, and nothing you enter is sent to a server or saved.
